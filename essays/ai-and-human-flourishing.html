AI and Human Flourishing

The most compelling question about artificial intelligence today isn't whether the models are impressive—it's whether they actually expand our capacity to think, act, and cooperate. In my notes from recent months, I keep returning to a single fork in the road: AI can become a prosthetic for human agency, or it can settle in as another layer of dependence that quietly narrows the space of our lives.

Tools Should Increase Agency, Not Replace It

AI tools ought to augment human capabilities and decision-making, not supplant them. This principle is essential for both individual flourishing and organizational health. In many workplaces, however, the introduction of AI triggers a reflexive tightening of control. A recent office drama over EU AI compliance illustrates this. An overzealous colleague framed GitHub Copilot as a unique risk—arguing that code could cause blackouts while emails cannot—despite Microsoft Copilot having already passed regulatory approvals. The real issue, as the narrator counters, is not compliance but how teams verify and review code. Overbearing policies that attempt to centralize control over AI deployment risk stripping teams of the agency to develop context-sensitive workflows. When tools are treated as threats to be managed rather than extensions of human intent, they become replacements rather than amplifiers.

Conversely, personal uses of AI can dramatically increase agency. Consider the case of using PI (a conversational AI) to build a custom note-taking and idea-organizing system. By creating a folder of notes, having the AI analyze and break them into byte-sized insights, and even pulling valuable tweets to share on Bluesky, an individual can craft a personalized creative pipeline. This is AI as a force multiplier for personal expression and productivity—exactly the kind of empowerment the principle demands.

The underlying human drive for security often gives rise to rigid systems that make life harder for everyone. If we instead designed systems where security is universal and the most vulnerable feel safe, individual and collective benefits would align. AI tools, when aligned with human agency, could help dissolve unnecessary bureaucracy and unleash creativity.

How do we strike a balance between necessary safeguards and the preservation of human agency? The answer will determine whether AI ultimately serves to elevate or to enmesh us.
