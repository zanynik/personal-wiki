Shared Living Economic Models

Exploration of how economic principles shape rent‑sharing and resource allocation in co‑living arrangements, focusing on fairness, dynamics of access, and the design of decision‑support tools.

Economic Dynamics of Shared Resources

The economic dynamics of shared resources in communal living often hinge on balancing market logic with interpersonal relationships. A proposed app for VEGA rent-sharing illustrates this tension: it would price room access based on features (e.g., backyard vs. front yard access) while accounting for social dynamics between residents. For instance, in a flat with three rooms offering backyard access, the cost to use these spaces might drop significantly if residents share good relationships, as trust reduces transactional friction. This creates a conflict between egalitarian pricing models and the reality of uneven social capital—friends may negotiate informal access, while non-friends face higher costs. The app’s core challenge lies in quantifying these "soft" dynamics (e.g., ease of knocking on a door) within a monetary framework. A key question arises: How do we reconcile subjective social equity with objective resource allocation in shared economies?
